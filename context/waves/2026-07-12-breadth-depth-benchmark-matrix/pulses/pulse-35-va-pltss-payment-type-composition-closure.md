# Pulse 35: VA PLTSS Payment-Type Composition Closure

## Objective

Resolve the highest-value internally actionable PLTSS methodology field while
keeping payment classification separate from debt and collection lineage.

## Evidence

Captured VA's FY2025 Agency Financial Report Section III under checksum-verified
custody. The PLTSS table is on PDF file page 24, printed page 139; the related
PLTSS narrative is on PDF file pages 28–29, printed pages 143–144.

The same-period PaymentAccuracy FY2024 annual row reports $218.30 million
overpayments, $6.41 million underpayments, $432.46 million technically improper
payments, and $102.93 million unknown payments. The first three reconcile to
$657.17 million improper payments, while improper plus unknown reconciles to
$760.10 million. This complete same-period composition is the primary closure
basis.

VA states that the later FY2025 reporting tested payments made in FY2024. Its table reports
$7,330.11 million outlays, $6,982.69 million proper payments, $301.58 million
improper payments, $77.08 million overpayments, $1.82 million underpayments,
$222.68 million technically improper payments, $45.84 million unknown payments,
and $347.41 million improper plus unknown payments.

## Decision

Close the FY2024 PLTSS `payment type split` field internally using the
same-period annual row. Use the FY2025 AFR only as later-cycle corroboration of
the category taxonomy. Aggregate methodology coverage moves from one of eight
to two of eight fields closed; six fields remain open.

Do not reconcile this composition to the earlier FY2024 scorecard estimate,
which used an October 2022 through September 2023 sample. The periods and
reported amounts differ.

## Boundary and next action

The report says incorrect-amount claims may lead to additional payments or
bills of collection, but it does not link table estimates or documentation
defects to bills, appeals, write-offs, collections, or collectible debt.
Technically improper and unknown categories are not recoverable amounts by
inference, and the $77.08 million overpayment estimate is not a debt ledger.

Keep scoring and all public recovery, savings, fraud, waste, debt, and
collectibility claims blocked. The next priority remains documentation defect
versus recoverable-overpayment basis, followed by claim-universe, estimator,
exclusion, sample-design, and documentation-standard evidence.
