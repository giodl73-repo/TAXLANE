# Pulse 18: Pell Short-Training Impact Evidence

## Result

Attached the Institute of Education Sciences randomized evaluation of two
experimental Pell Grant eligibility expansions for short occupational
training. Both offers increased study-school enrollment and completion, but
neither produced a statistically detectable medium- to long-term employment
or quarterly earnings effect. The record preserves intent-to-treat status,
sample and outcome periods, standard errors, p-values, grant take-up, and
disbursement context.

## Decision gate

Pass for causal effects of offering experimental Pell eligibility to the
study's eligible, interested applicants at volunteer schools. Fail for effects
of grant use, enrollment, completion, or credentials themselves; proof of harm
or equivalence from nonsignificant estimates; transport to all students or
programs; a complete cost-effectiveness calculation; current fiscal effects;
fraud; or recoverable savings.

## Next bounded action

Find a current education-to-employment evaluation with both a randomized or
credible quasi-experimental counterfactual and a complete cohort-compatible
incremental cost analysis before attempting cost-effectiveness or fiscal
claims.
