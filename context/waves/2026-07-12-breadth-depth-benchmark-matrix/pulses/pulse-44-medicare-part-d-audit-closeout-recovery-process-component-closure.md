# Pulse 44: Medicare Part D Audit-Closeout Recovery-Process Component Closure

## Objective

Determine whether the captured Part D scorecard supports a narrow recovery-
process component without treating the current process as the recoverable basis
for the FY2024 estimate based on CY2022 payments.

## Evidence

The Q4 2025 scorecard says CMS conducted plan-sponsor audits focused on drugs at
high risk of overpayment. Its recovery-plan table identifies `Recovery Audit` as
the method and reports issued closeout notices for the Adcirca, Revatio, and
Cialis national audit. Those notices require sponsors to delete PDE records
determined improper under Medicare Part D, which the publisher describes as
resulting in recovery of the payments to the program.

The table separately identifies planned closeout notifications for the Durable
Medical Equipment and Tepezza national audits. Planned actions are not treated
as already issued or completed.

## Decision

Close one internal component: the current scorecard documents an audit-closeout
pathway from audit determination through required PDE deletion to publisher-
described recovery. Keep the full `overpayment versus recoverable amount basis`
field open.

The Q4 2025 process is later than the FY2024/CY2022 estimate and has no PDE,
cohort, amount, or period linkage to that estimate. It cannot support a recovery
percentage or recoverable-dollar amount.

## Residuals and guardrails

Still missing are PDE identifiers, counts, and amounts; linkage to the IPM
sample or estimate; corrected-GDC-to-recoverable-payment calculation; debt,
notice, dispute, appeal, and reversal treatment; deletion-to-reconciliation,
offset, receivable, refund, or cash mechanics; recoverable, collectible,
collected, waived, compromised, written-off, and outstanding amounts; liability
allocation; coverage across causes and audits; and control cost or net savings.

“Resulting in recovery” remains publisher process language, not proof of cash
received. Issued notices remain distinct from planned notices. PDE deletion does
not establish debt, collectibility, cash collection, or full federal recovery.
Gross Drug Cost combines plan and beneficiary liability, and no source maps it
to recovered federal amounts. Fraud, waste, and abuse education language is not
a fraud or waste finding.

This pulse adds one component closure and zero full-field closures. Part D
remains three fields closed and five open. Every public, field-closure, scoring,
fraud, waste, debt, collectibility, recovery, prevention, and savings gate
remains false.
