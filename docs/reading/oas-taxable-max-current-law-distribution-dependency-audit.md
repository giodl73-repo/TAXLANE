# OAS taxable-maximum current-law and distribution dependency audit

OAS advances from two to three evidence-ready gates because the CBO option page
does publish bounded distribution results. For retired workers claiming at age
65, it reports benefit and lifetime payroll-tax effects by lifetime household
earnings quintile for the 1960s, 1970s, and 1980s birth cohorts. That evidence
was previously overlooked.

The boundary matters. Those tables do not resolve survivors, disabled workers,
families, poverty, employer compensation shifts, self-employed administration,
or payroll-system transition. Nor is the option current law: the 2026 taxable
maximum remains $184,500 and the latest observed taxable ratio is 83.3 percent,
not the candidate's 90-percent target.

CBO's vintage estimate improves the 75-year actuarial balance by 0.9 percent of
taxable payroll and delays combined-fund depletion from 2034 to 2037, but does
not achieve full solvency. The current 2026 Trustees deficit is 4.42 percent of
taxable payroll, so a matching rescore remains necessary. OAS stays off the
primary-spending and REV rails, with zero admission. The next action is to
rerank the remaining owner-outcome lanes.
