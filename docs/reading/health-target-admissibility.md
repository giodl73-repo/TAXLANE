# When A Medicare-Relative Target Is Admissible

Machine record:
`data/derived/breadth_benchmark_matrix/health_target_admissibility.v1.draft.json`.

## Decision

Medicare prices are admissible as a **conditional scenario anchor** for hospital
and professional services. They are not admissible as a universal target.

## Why

| Test | Hospital evidence | Professional-service evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial reference | 253% of Medicare | 139% of Medicare |
| Access | MedPAC says beneficiary hospital access remained good overall. | MedPAC has generally found Medicare clinician access as good as or better than privately insured access. |
| Adequacy | FY2024 aggregate Medicare margin −12.1%; median relatively efficient hospital −1%; projected efficient-hospital median +1% in 2026. | Medicare remains a viable fee-schedule reference, but MedPAC recommends updates and more accurate relative rates. |
| Quality | Mixed; access alone is not an outcome guarantee. | Must be tested by service, specialty, geography, and beneficiary group. |

The hospital evidence cuts both ways, which is exactly why it is useful. Near
break-even results for relatively efficient hospitals make Medicare a credible
scenario anchor. The deeply negative aggregate Medicare margin makes a blanket
100% target indefensible without adjustments and transition rules.

## Required Floors

A scenario is not scoreable until it:

- adjusts for geography, wages, teaching, uncompensated care, case mix, and
  safety-net role;
- treats rural, critical-access, and low-volume providers separately;
- preserves beneficiary access, waiting time, networks, and clinician
  participation;
- preserves or improves risk-adjusted mortality, readmission, patient
  experience, and appropriate utilization; and
- includes monitoring and a stop-or-revise rule when a floor fails.

## What Comes Next

Build low, central, and high Medicare-relative scenarios by service and provider
class. Each scenario must show gross payment effects, transition cost,
behavioral response, access/quality results, and net fiscal effect separately.

```text
credible anchor != universal target != gross reduction != net savings
```

Source: MedPAC, *March 2026 Report to the Congress: Medicare Payment Policy*;
RAND Hospital Price Transparency Study Round 5.1; Milliman commercial
reimbursement benchmark.
