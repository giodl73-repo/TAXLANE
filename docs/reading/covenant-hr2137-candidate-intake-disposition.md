# COVENANT's first real candidate enters Taxlane—and stays held

COVENANT now produces a replayable official candidate pack for H.R. 2137, the
Review Every Veterans Claim Act of 2025. This is the first of the four new
domain owners to move beyond a candidate-free official baseline.

| FY2026-FY2035 component | CBO outlays |
|---|---:|
| Claims, appeals, IT, studies, and court changes | +$173M |
| Pension and Medicaid interaction | -$145M |
| Combined effect | +$28M |

The annual path costs $49M in FY2026 and first turns negative in FY2032. That
later reduction is driven by the statutory pension/Medicaid interaction, not
evidence that claims processing became more efficient.

## What changed

- Taxlane can replay a non-null candidate from its exact producer commit.
- The producer independently reconciles the annual score and component split.
- The pack identifies the current-law null, affected appeals workload, staffing
  assumptions, TEF/Medicaid overlap, uncertainty, and missing floors.
- The candidate identity matches Taxlane's existing H.R. 2137 record; it is
  corroboration, not a second candidate or a second fiscal effect.

## Why VET still does not reopen

The bill is reported in the House but not enacted. The pack has no observed or
defensible policy/stress results for timeliness, accuracy, appeal rights,
beneficiary incidence, backlog resilience, or delivery capacity. It also lacks
executed appropriations and a separately identified implementation/transition
cost path.

Taxlane therefore custodies the pack but admits no FY2026 reduction. The
$813.727B target and 21/23/33/35/43/46/48 planning schedule do not change.
