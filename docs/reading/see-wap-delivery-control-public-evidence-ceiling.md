# SEE WAP delivery-control evidence ceiling

## The controls already have owners

DOE accepted all three 2024 OIG recommendations and described corrective
actions for average unit cost, late reporting, monitoring capacity, and funded
grantees with no reported completed units. OIG considered those responses
responsive. The planned completion dates fell in 2025.

The 2026 Wisconsin audit similarly has responsive actions for cost records,
conflicts, performance evaluation, and complaints. Some work had begun by the
audit, but the report does not establish full closeout.

Creating a second control program would therefore duplicate work already
assigned to DOE, grantees, and OIG. The useful Taxlane object is a verification
contract: did unit cost normalize, did reporting become timely, did completed
homes catch up, did corrective actions close, and did service remain intact?

## Why there is still no savings number

The programwide metrics largely describe 2023, and the Wisconsin scope covers
fiscal years 2022 through 2024. Public sources do not provide current
programwide corrective-action results, Wisconsin closeout, or quantified
questioned or disallowed costs. A control risk cannot be converted into cash.

Service recovery and avoided risk can eventually be reported as separate
benefits. Cashable savings additionally require allowable-cost disposition,
the budget period, fund disposition, and a same-cohort or matched effect.

## Portfolio consequence

SEE moves to named-trigger monitoring with zero admission. The next active lane
is EDU's post-2025 Pell current-law and household-access audit.
