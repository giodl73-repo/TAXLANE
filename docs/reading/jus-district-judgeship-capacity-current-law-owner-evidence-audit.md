# JUS District Judgeship Capacity Current-Law Audit

H.R. 1702 remains an unenacted capacity proposal. It would add 65 permanent
district judgeships and one temporary judgeship in staggered waves. CBO
estimated $111 million of direct judge-compensation outlays and $283 million of
appropriated operating outlays through 2035, but assumed enactment in FY2025.
That timing did not occur, so the $394 million path is historical proposal
context rather than a current-law forecast.

The current operating system still has a real capacity question. On July 28,
2026, the district courts had 677 authorized judgeships, 25 vacancies, and four
pending nominees. The 2025 Judicial Conference recommendation sought 69 new
permanent district judgeships for 25 courts; H.R. 1702 overlapped 24 of those
25 courts but is not the same package.

National FY2025 filings rose 6 percent to 382,692. Terminations exceeded
filings and pending matters fell 17 percent to 541,945, yet the civil median
time rose from 13.7 to 15.6 months. Those aggregate movements are materially
affected by case mix and multidistrict litigation and cannot identify the
effect of adding judgeships. The Texas Southern snapshot remains a useful
local stress signal, not a candidate effect.

JUS therefore selects a district-capacity delivery and caseflow stress as its
next public-evidence step. It must preserve appointment, confirmation,
staffing, facilities, security, technology, senior and magistrate assistance,
case mix, counsel, liberty, victim, disparity, and durable-resolution
boundaries. Zero spending or revenue reduction is admitted and no rates change.
