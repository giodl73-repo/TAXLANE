# DEF Level-4 force, readiness, and allocation audit

The broad $959 billion defense option does not pass package admission. All eight
required gates remain blocked because the option does not select an
implementation across force structure, readiness, personnel, procurement,
strategic capacity, allied burden, transition cost, and the updated accounting
perimeter.

This is a specificity failure, not a finding that all defense reductions are
infeasible. CBO itself describes several materially different implementations,
including a smaller active force, a different unit mix, or greater allied
provision, but its score does not detail an alternative. Each choice has
different mission, distribution, procurement, and transition effects.

Current official evidence reinforces the need for a program-level allocation.
GAO reports delayed acquisition milestones and immature technologies in major
programs, plus critical operating-and-support cost growth in 14 of 36 reviewed
systems. Those findings identify execution risk; they do not establish a
recoverable-savings amount.

The next valid DEF candidate must select a mission and force case, allocate the
annual reductions by appropriation and program, protect reviewed readiness
floors, cost transition and execution risk, and obtain a matched current-law
score.
