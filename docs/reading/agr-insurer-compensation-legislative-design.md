# AGR insurer-compensation legislative design

## What this design changes

The public-evidence ceiling showed that USDA cannot create the intended net
savings merely by renegotiating the Standard Reinsurance Agreement. This
design therefore states the legislative change directly: Congress would need
to permit prospective underwriting-gain savings to leave the insurer-payment
pool and reduce mandatory outlays.

The initial specification does not reduce producer premium support or A&O
delivery reimbursement. It isolates underwriting returns so that a fiscal test
does not silently become a producer-access or agent-service cut.

## The scoreable case

`UW12_PROSPECTIVE` tests a 12-percent expected long-run return on retained
premium, phased in at 25, 50, and 100 percent over three reinsurance years. The
parameter follows an earlier CBO option and makes the specification
reproducible. It does not establish that 12 percent is the correct current
market return. A separately reproduced market comparator remains a sensitivity
case, and current law remains the null case.

The model must report gross term effects, federal implementation cost,
behavioral and service offsets, stabilization payments, and net mandatory
outlays separately. It must use a matching current-law baseline that includes
Public Law 119-21 and the 2026 SRA.

## Service before savings

Five fail-closed floors protect nationwide availability, provider capacity,
specialty and high-loss service, written-agreement quality, and claims and
appeals. A breached floor pauses the affected adjustment and can trigger
targeted, scored stabilization. Missing measurement is not a pass.

The design freezes a three-year preimplementation baseline, reports annually,
evaluates independently every two reinsurance years, and recalibrates or
reauthorizes after five. Its possible states are continue, pause, stabilize,
recalibrate, or terminate and return to current law.

## What happens next

The next artifact is a reproducible sensitivity and service-stress envelope.
It can show how the mechanism behaves under explicit assumptions, but its
outputs remain illustrative until current owner market data, service-floor
evidence, and a matching current-law score exist. No savings or tax rate changes
are admitted here.
