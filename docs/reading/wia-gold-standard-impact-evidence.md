# WIA Gold Standard Impact Evidence

Machine record:
`data/derived/breadth_benchmark_matrix/wia_gold_standard_impact_evidence.2011-2013.v1.draft.json`.

The Department of Labor's WIA Gold Standard Evaluation supplies the first
causal worker-services layer in this branch. It randomized 35,665 applicants
in 28 local workforce investment areas between November 2011 and April 2013.
The impact-study sample contained 34,429 people; the National Directory of New
Hires (NDNH) analysis used 33,773. Assignment was 88 percent to full WIA
services, 6 percent to core plus intensive services, and 6 percent to core
services only.

The restricted groups could not receive the higher WIA-funded service tiers
for the first 15 months. Outcomes came from a 30-month survey and 36 months of
NDNH administrative earnings. The estimates are intent-to-treat effects of
access to a service tier, not effects among only the people who used it.

| Cumulative earnings, 2012 dollars | Full WIA | Core + intensive | Core only | Intensive contrast | Training contrast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30-month survey | $39,528 | $43,211 | $36,079 | +$7,133, significant under the strict standard | -$3,684, not significant |
| 36-month NDNH | $46,509 | $47,960 | $44,664 | +$3,296, conventionally significant but not under the strict standard | -$1,451, not significant |

The intensive contrast compares core-plus-intensive with core-only access. The
training contrast compares full WIA with core-plus-intensive access. Survey
and NDNH values use different sources, samples, and follow-up periods and
therefore should not be pooled.

## Receipt and Credentials

| Reported percentage | Full WIA | Core + intensive | Core only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Received training | 50% | 41% | 34% |
| Received one-on-one services | 63% | 61% | 47% |
| Earned any credential | 29% | 24% | 15% |
| Completed vocational training | 36% | 28% | 21% |
| Earned a vocational-training credential | 27% | 22% | 14% |

The training take-up difference between full WIA and core plus intensive was
only nine percentage points. Customers could cross over or obtain training
outside WIA, and the access restriction ended after 15 months. DOL therefore
characterizes the training impact evidence as inconclusive. The negative,
statistically nonsignificant earnings contrasts are not proof of harm.

## Benefit-Cost Perspectives

The report's benefit-cost values are per customer in 2012 dollars. Perspective
order matters: society, customers, then taxpayers.

| Service layer | Society | Customers | Taxpayers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training | -$5,046 | -$3,274 | -$1,773 |
| Intensive | +$8,573 | +$6,630 | +$1,943 |
| Intensive and training combined | +$3,526 | +$3,356 | +$170 |

The customer and taxpayer combined values equal their published components.
The society components sum to $3,527, one dollar above the published $3,526
combined value, a retained whole-dollar rounding difference. These are
historical benefit-cost estimates, not recoverable savings or current budget
effects.

## Evidence Boundary

Random assignment supports causal conclusions for the study's historical WIA
service-access contrasts. It does not eliminate transportability limits. WIA
was the predecessor to WIOA, study enrollment occurred in 2011-2013, the
restriction lasted only 15 months, and veterans and other excluded applicants
are outside the direct impact population. Program rules, service delivery,
labor markets, and funding have since changed.

The evaluation cannot be joined mechanically to FY2025 OMB subfunction 504
outlays. It supplies no cohort-compatible present-day outlay crosswalk and
does not measure fraud. Current cost per outcome, recoverable savings, and a
current fiscal score remain blocked.
