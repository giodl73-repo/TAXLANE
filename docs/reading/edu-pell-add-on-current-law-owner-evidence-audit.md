# EDU Pell Add-On: What Survived, and What Did Not

The candidate survived the current-law identity check. For 2026–27, the maximum Pell
Grant remains $7,395, and CBO's post-reconciliation description still decomposes that
amount into a $6,335 discretionary maximum and a $1,060 mandatory add-on. Eliminating
the add-on would therefore reduce the maximum scheduled award by 14.334 percent.

The old score did not survive as a current estimate. CBO's December 2024 option began
in July 2025 and estimated $44.4 billion less mandatory outlays plus $0.8 billion less
discretionary outlays through 2034. Since then, enacted law changed Pell eligibility,
the 2026 appropriation fixed a new award year, and Workforce Pell took effect. The
$45.2 billion combined figure is historical option context, not an admitted saving.

Existing TAXLANE evidence is useful but does not close the decision. Administrative
data establishes program scale, not a common fiscal-recipient cohort. The BPS entrant
comparison is descriptive and immature. Randomized short-training offers increased
enrollment and completion in their study settings, but they do not estimate a national
award cut. Historical bachelor-completer borrowing cannot identify loan substitution.

The next executable slice is a current 2026–27 recipient and institution distribution
with a bounded loan and unmet-need substitution stress. Until that exists alongside a
compatible rescore, EDU contributes zero savings and cannot change a rate.
