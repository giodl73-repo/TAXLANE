NAEP Proficient is not grade-level proficiency, and NAEP says achievement
levels should be interpreted with caution. Percentages are separately rounded:
for grade-4 mathematics, the displayed 31% Proficient and 9% Advanced shares
sum to 40%, while the separately calculated published headline is 39% at or
above Proficient.

The latest completion measure attached here is deliberately period-labeled.
In school year 2021–22, the public-school four-year adjusted cohort graduation
rate was 87%. Rates were 94% for Asian/Pacific Islander students, 90% for White
students, 83% for Hispanic students, 81% for Black students, and 74% for
American Indian/Alaska Native students. Selected rates were 71% for students
with disabilities, 72% for English learners, 81% for economically disadvantaged
students, and 68% for enrolled homeless students.

## Attendance And Access

The Department of Education's school year 2022–23 files report 13,406,900
chronically absent students over 48,268,200 membership, or **27.8%**. Chronic
absence means missing at least 10% of school days. Reported rates were 36% for
children with disabilities versus 26% without disabilities, and 33% for
English learners versus 27% for non-English learners.

Race/ethnicity rates ranged from 15% for Asian students and 22% for White
students to 45% for Pacific Islander students and 46% for American Indian
students; Black, Hispanic, and students of two or more races were 37%, 33%, and
30%, respectively. These are descriptive distributions, not causal findings.
National aggregation can count a mobile student more than once, membership is
measured at a point in time while absence accumulates over the year, and state
definitions and data quality vary.

The finance frame is FY2024, NAEP was administered in early 2024, attendance is
for 2022–23, and the ACGR is for 2021–22. These are neither the same cohort nor
perfectly identical institutional scopes. The [PISA 2022 Peer Comparison](k12-pisa-2022-peer-comparison.md)
adds a definition-matched international outcome comparison for 15-year-olds,
but it is not matched to the finance frame and cannot be crosswalked to NAEP
achievement levels. The [Census CPS access and transition baseline](census-cps-education-access-transition-baseline.md)
adds household-survey age enrollment and recent-graduate context. Its civilian
noninstitutionalized population is not the Education Department's public-school
membership or four-year ACGR cohort. Additional program-compatible access
denominators remain open. Do not divide spending by a proficiency, attendance,
or enrollment percentage or infer an expected spending level, waste, fraud, or
savings from this baseline.
