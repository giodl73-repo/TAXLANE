## Current-Law Baseline

CBO's February 2026 baseline projects:

- debt held by the public rising from **101% of GDP in 2026** to **120% in 2036**;
- the total deficit reaching **6.7% of GDP** in 2036;
- net interest reaching **4.6% of GDP**; and
- the primary deficit remaining **2.1% of GDP**.

The machine-readable CBO vintage reports a $3.115T deficit and $46.712T of
nominal GDP in 2036; the rounded public headline remains 6.7% of GDP.

These are annual FY2036 arithmetic equivalents, not ten-year budget scores.
They can be divided into illustrative revenue-heavy (75/25), balanced (50/50),
or primary-outlay-heavy (25/75) mixes. Those mixes are not recommendations.

## Why Primary Balance Is Not The Same As Stable Debt

Debt-to-GDP also depends on the effective interest rate, nominal economic
growth, starting debt, the entire phase-in path, and financing adjustments not
recorded in the primary deficit. A zero primary balance therefore does not
automatically stabilize debt, especially with debt already near the size of the
economy and net interest rising.

Interest remains a legal financing cost and is paid in every path. It is not
booked as a program cut. Before policy scoring, each path needs distribution,
timing, macroeconomic feedback, lawful-commitment, and outcome analysis.

```text
annual adjustment equivalent != ten-year score != debt stabilization
```
