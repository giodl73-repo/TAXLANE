# DIS NFIP Repetitive-Loss Mitigation Stress Envelope

Targeting repetitive-loss properties is plausible, but the public record does
not support a national savings number. The 48 percent claims concentration is
historical and includes losses that mitigation may not prevent. FEMA publishes
benefit-cost and cost-share rules, selection thresholds, and project lists; it
does not publish the current same-property cohort needed to join intervention
cost, completion, post-mitigation claims, premium changes, retained coverage,
other aid, and federal fund timing.

The `taxlane-dis-flood` feature crate makes the missing relationships visible.
Its 100-property demonstration has 50 participants and 40 completions, a
three-year lag, $100,000 assumed unit cost, 75 percent assumed federal share,
and seven active benefit years. With explicitly hypothetical claim, premium,
aid, and administration inputs, it produces a $3.0 million investment,
$1.4 million of avoided claims, $280,000 of other-aid reduction, $140,000 of
lost premium revenue, $70,000 of administration, and a negative $1.53 million
net federal effect. The break-even expected-claims input is $20,928.57 per
completed property per active year at the assumed 50 percent avoidance rate.

Those figures are a unit test of the accounting, not a proposal forecast.
Participation, completion, timing, insurance revenue, and aid interaction can
reverse the result. FEMA's benefit-cost ratio also includes social benefits
outside this narrower federal budget perimeter.

DIS therefore moves to named triggers for a current linkable property cohort
or an official score. It contributes zero admitted savings and no rate change;
JUS becomes the next owner/outcome lane.
