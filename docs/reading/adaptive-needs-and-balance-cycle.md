# Adaptive needs and balance cycle

Taxlane should not try to freeze the United States at another country's budget
percentages. A useful budget repeatedly balances durable commitments with the
most serious current pains, tests whether interventions work, and reallocates
again.

## The recurring motion

**Measure → protect floors → diagnose pain and excess → rank interventions →
allocate → deliver and monitor → sunset or continue → rebalance.**

This is deliberately cyclical. A transportation backlog may justify temporary
repair funding this cycle and little additional funding after it clears. A
health-price reform may lower cost without reducing access. A disaster reserve
may replenish after a shock. Education receives additional money only when a
specific education pain and intervention outrank alternatives—not because the
lane appeared in an illustrative profile.

## What “needy” means

The comparison is not a single political score. It asks whether a floor is at
risk; how severe, widespread, unequal, urgent, and reversible the harm is; what
the next dollar is expected to improve; how strong that evidence is; whether
the system can deliver; whether costs move to households or other governments;
and whether the funding is fiscally durable. Peer and historical ranges help
detect anomalies but never decide the allocation alone.

## Structural money versus repair money

| Funding type | Appropriate use | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Structural base | Durable obligation or continuing high-value service | Annual performance plus multiyear review |
| Temporary pain response | Backlog, shock, repair, transition, or acute capacity gap | Milestones and automatic sunset |
| Evidence-building pilot | Plausible intervention with material uncertainty | Predefined scale-or-stop evaluation |
| Stabilization reserve | Disaster, downturn, or other contingent exposure | Trigger and replenishment rules |

The four profiles now reserve $75B, $150B, $250B, or $350B for this process.
They do not pre-commit the reserve to education or any other lane. That change
does not alter the profiles' net-savings totals or conditional rate schedules.

## First all-lane diagnostic pass

The first pass now classifies a concrete problem and plausible response type
for every track. Examples include health cost pressure with access protection,
retirement solvency and adequacy, child poverty and family-support gaps,
veteran claims backlog, transportation safety and asset condition, disaster
exposure, and debt-service pressure. Education is classified for targeted or
pilot interventions, not a blanket topline increase.

This is an intervention-design queue, not a funding ranking. The indicators
have different units, years, affected populations, and causal relationships.
Accordingly, cross-lane need scores and dollar allocations remain null until a
common observation window, reviewed severity thresholds, and intervention-
specific marginal benefits and costs are available.

## How a future allocation is calculated

An intervention must clear ten gates before it receives a score. It needs a
current pain measure and affected population, a severity target distinct from
the baseline, a specific owner and intervention, a bounded marginal outcome,
whole-system cost and cost-shift analysis, distribution, delivery capacity,
overlap review, a duration and sunset rule, and policy/stress floor passage.

The disclosed primary score weights pain severity and marginal outcome at 25%
each; affected population and urgency at 15% each; and equity and whole-system
net value at 10% each. Evidence strength and delivery confidence then discount
the result. Equal-weight, pain-heavy, and outcome-heavy sensitivity cases test
whether the conclusion depends on the normative weights.

The allocator holds 10% as contingency, caps a single lane at 30% of the
allocatable reserve, respects intervention-specific capacity caps, and leaves
unallocatable money in reserve. The first audit admits zero lanes, so all four
profile reserves remain wholly unallocated. This is an evidence-readiness
result, not a conclusion that no needs exist.

## First intervention dossiers

Three pain-aligned dossiers now exercise the ten gates:

- **VET:** an accountable VA claims-processing modernization bundle using
  named claims systems, training controls, and AI accountability controls
  against the 68,207-claim backlog probe. Candidate definition is ready, but
  the count remains claims rather than unique veterans. VA's June 2026
  71.3-day average completion time adds a second service baseline, not an
  incremental causal throughput effect or complete cost.
- **TRN:** systemic centerline and shoulder rumble strips on eligible rural
  two-lane corridors against the 2024 rate of 1.19 fatalities per 100 million
  vehicle miles and 39,254 reported deaths. FHWA supplies bounded crash-subset
  effects and a historical planning input of approximately $3,800 per mile for
  combined centerline and shoulder lines with a seven-year life. Eligible
  exposure, current bid prices, maintenance, state/local shares,
  accommodations, delivery capacity, and stress results remain open.
  Custodied FHWA tables now narrow the first screen to 894,715 rural
  nonfreeway arterial-and-collector route-miles and 11,790 fatalities on those
  systems. Those are still all lane configurations and crash types—not the
  eligible denominator or affected numerator.
  The national 2024 HPMS section filter then returns 753,112.2968 rural,
  two-way, two-through-lane section-miles across all 50 states. Because FHWA's
  spatial full-join totals do not reconcile directly to HM-20, applying the
  within-system shares to official route miles yields a separate
  687,819.6543-mile analytical screen. Neither result clears the design-
  eligibility gate.
  A further HPMS availability audit finds that 96.219105% of filtered
  section-miles lack one or both reported shoulder widths and 71.812146% lack
  IRI. The 4,112.3005 miles reporting at least four feet on both sides form a
  local-review queue, not a national eligibility estimate: FHWA's reference is
  usable width beyond a proposed strip, modified designs may be possible, and
  IRI does not establish pavement structure. Missing data are not classified
  as failure. Existing-treatment inventories, detailed pavement and geometry,
  user and noise accommodations, and target-crash records must come from road
  owners before national cost or outcome scoring.
  The first road-owner pilot then captures PennDOT's official statewide 2024
  workbook: 57,397 treatment rows across all 67 counties. Its normalized
  two-way, two-lane interval union is 5,702.436553 miles, but this is an
  analytical treatment footprint rather than comparable rural route-miles.
  It is therefore not subtracted from Pennsylvania's 20,851.0429-mile HPMS
  screen. A PennDOT RMS/LRS join must first align county, route, segment,
  direction, offsets, functional class, ownership, pavement, geometry, and
  crashes and report unmatched records. This is the reusable state-inventory
  protocol; it is not permission to extrapolate Pennsylvania nationally.
  Public RMS segment, administration, and shoulder services now execute the
  join without an external request. Of the candidate-setting inventory rows,
  99.910259% match an exact roadway segment and 95.044002% match one
  administration interval. The reconciled state-owned, rural, two-way,
  two-lane, FHWA-class 3–6 treatment footprint is 4,401.040152 miles. That is
  the first comparable treated footprint, not an untreated estimate: the API
  capture was intentionally limited to county-route pairs containing a known
  treatment. At that stage, a valid subtraction still required a denominator
  containing every state-owned route, including those with no workbook record.
  That complete same-basis PennDOT screen is now captured. It contains
  20,130.978409 state-owned rural, two-way, two-lane, FHWA-class 3–6 miles;
  splitting at administration boundaries and unioning treatments yields
  4,595.868561 treated miles and 15,535.109848 untreated screening miles.
  PennDOT and HPMS are not forced to agree because the former is state-owned
  and the latter is all-public. The untreated value is a screening remainder,
  not design-eligible mileage: pavement structure, side-specific usable
  shoulders, bicycle and noise constraints, programmed projects, target
  crashes, current prices, maintenance, and delivery capacity remain open.
  A complete 124,096-record state-jurisdiction shoulder capture now divides
  that untreated screen by field availability. Unambiguous current paved-width
  records show at least four feet on both sides for 2,577.521402 miles and one
  or both sides below four feet for 12,901.123674 miles; 55.676515 miles lack a
  side record and 0.788258 have missing or conflicting current widths. This
  narrows the local-review queue but is not a design decision: reported paved
  width is not verified usable clear width beyond a proposed strip.
  Segment-level pavement fields then form an engineering-record priority
  queue. Good or excellent reported OPI overlaps the both-sides four-foot
  paved-width band for 1,631.432386 miles; a 2021–2026 surface year overlaps
  that width band for 1,163.282197 miles. Neither intersection is design-
  eligible mileage. Public RMS does not provide pavement thickness, cracking,
  base structure, remaining structural life, committed resurfacing, or project
  schedules, and its separate pavement-condition-rating field is missing
  throughout the untreated screen.
- **ISF:** a one-year ARPA-style advance refundable Child Tax Credit
  specification against the 2024 14.3% under-18 official poverty rate and
  10.35 million children below poverty. Official historical evidence supports
  design, outcome, and delivery investigation. The TY2026 comparator is now
  explicit: a $2,200 maximum credit, no more than $1,700 refundable, an
  under-17 age rule, and earnings-conditioned refundability. That still cannot
  replace a current-law cost, distribution, interaction, and transferability
  analysis of the candidate delta.

The result is advancement, not admission: VET clears 1 of 10 gates, TRN 2, and
ISF 3. TRN's cost gate advances from blocked to partial. Reviewed official web sources are identified but are not yet raw,
checksummed repository custody. Every score and reserve award therefore remains
null.

The dossiers also reject convenient but incorrect substitutions: H.R. 2137's
pension-limit and Medicaid score is not claims-backlog productivity, and H.R.
2247's airmen-certificate cost note is not roadway safety.
