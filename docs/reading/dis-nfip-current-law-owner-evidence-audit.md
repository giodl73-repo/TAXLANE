# DIS NFIP: Replace the Old Premium Option with Risk Reduction

The selected 2018 CBO option was specific: end pre-FIRM discounts, eliminate
two statutory surcharges, and raise the reserve assessment. CBO estimated $1.3
billion of lower outlays over 2019–2028 and identified property counts and
coverage take-up as uncertainties. TAXLANE's prior rounded $1.0 billion label is
replaced by that exact source value, but neither amount is current or admitted.

Risk Rating 2.0 changed the operating perimeter. The full book moved to the new
property-specific approach in April 2023, while statutory annual caps keep many
policies on a glidepath. GAO estimated a $26.7 billion premium shortfall through
2037, but that is neither a cash appropriation nor an available saving. Immediate
full-risk pricing without targeted assistance could cause people to drop
coverage and rely more heavily on disaster aid.

The stronger successor attacks physical risk. In January 2026, 112,640 insured
repetitive-loss properties represented about 2.5 percent of NFIP policies;
properties with two or more losses had received 48 percent of historical claim
dollars as of December 2021. Concentration identifies where to investigate—it
does not say 48 percent is avoidable.

Targeted acquisition, elevation, relocation, or floodproofing can reduce claims
and premiums, but it requires investment, typically a nonfederal contribution,
voluntary participation, and years of delivery. The next slice must join those
costs and timelines to same-property claim, premium, coverage, and aid outcomes.
Until then, DIS contributes zero savings and no rate change.
