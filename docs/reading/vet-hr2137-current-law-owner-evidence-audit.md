# VET H.R. 2137 Current-Law Owner-Evidence Audit

H.R. 2137 remains ordered reported in the House but unenacted. CBO assumed
enactment near the beginning of FY2026. That did not happen, so its annual
FY2026–35 path is historical proposal context and requires a rescore if the
bill advances.

The proposal is also not a simple claims-efficiency measure. CBO estimated
$144 million for claims and appeals process changes, including IT, appeals
aggregation, review of claims after missed examinations, and reports. Court
jurisdiction adds further cost. The late direct-spending reduction instead
comes from extending reduced VA pensions for veterans and survivors in
Medicaid nursing homes: VA payments fall by roughly $10 million per month while
Medicaid costs rise by roughly $6 million per month.

CBO's complete path records $108 million less direct spending and $136 million
more appropriated outlays through 2035—a combined $28 million cost, including a
$49 million FY2026 cost. The pension/Medicaid interaction is already inside the
direct path and cannot be relabeled or counted again as claims savings.

The current claims system supplies a strong baseline but no candidate effect:
600,878 rating claims in inventory, 69,481 older than 125 days, 83.62 percent
three-month whole-claim accuracy, and direct, evidence, and hearing appeal
dockets with materially different pending times. Current GAO work also keeps
exam quality, training, IT modernization, and measurement gaps open.

VET therefore selects the missed-exam evidence-review and accommodation
mechanism for a normalized workload, delay, correction, appeal, and veteran-
burden stress. Zero savings are admitted and no rate changes.
