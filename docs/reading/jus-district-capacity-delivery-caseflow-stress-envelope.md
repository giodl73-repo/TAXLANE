# JUS District Capacity Delivery and Caseflow Stress Envelope

Creating a judgeship does not create an operating court by itself. The new
feature engine carries proposed seats through appointment, supporting staff,
facilities/security/technology, and productive-capacity gates before any
mechanical caseflow contribution appears.

In the transparent partial-delivery fixture, ten proposed seats become eight
appointed judges, 7.2 staffed judges, 5.4 ready judges, and 4.86 effective
judge-equivalents. An assumed 400 annual terminations per fully productive new
judge produces a 1,944-case stress contribution. Against 60,000 filings,
58,000 baseline terminations, and 20,000 beginning pending matters, ending
pending is 22,000 without the candidate and 20,056 in the stress case.

The same fixture separates $2.4 million of direct compensation, $5.04 million
of operating cost, and $1.0 million of one-time readiness cost. Its modeled
$4,341.56 per additional termination is only arithmetic on hypothetical
inputs. It is not a value finding because a termination does not establish
timeliness, accuracy, counsel, liberty, victim participation, equal treatment,
or durable resolution.

Authorization without appointments adds no effective capacity; full delivery
is a mechanical upper stress. Neither is a forecast. JUS moves to named
triggers for enactment, observed staffed-seat exposure, matched caseflow and
rights outcomes, and a current score. Zero savings are admitted; VET becomes
the next owner/outcome lane.
