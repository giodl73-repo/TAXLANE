Government expenditure per full-time-equivalent student from primary through
post-secondary non-tertiary education was **$14,603** in the United States and
**$12,438** across the OECD, in equivalent U.S. dollars converted using
purchasing-power parities. The U.S. amount was $2,165, or 17.4%, higher.

Governments supplied 92.4% of total U.S. funding at those levels after transfers
to the private sector, versus 90.4% across the OECD.

## Broader Context And Boundary

From primary through tertiary education, total expenditure was $20,387 per
student in the United States versus $15,022 across the OECD, including tertiary
research and development. The corresponding investment was 5.8% versus 4.7%
of GDP. Those figures are broader education-system context, not K–12-only
comparisons.

The resource data and PISA assessment both reference 2022, but this does not
make the spending causal for the tested students: annual institutional spending
is not assigned to their schools, programs, or cumulative prior inputs. The
OECD average is descriptive, not an automatic target. These OECD measures also
cannot be substituted for OMB federal function 500 or the Census school-system
frame. No expected spending level, waste, fraud, or savings is scored.
