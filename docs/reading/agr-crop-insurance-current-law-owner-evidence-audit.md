# AGR crop-insurance current-law and owner-evidence audit

AGR's December 2024 option contains two materially different interventions:
an across-the-board reduction in producer premium support and a reduction in
insurer administrative reimbursement and targeted return. Treating them as a
single $4.9 billion FY2026 savings number hides different service,
distribution, legal, and delivery risks.

## What current evidence establishes

- Public Law 119-21 changed crop-insurance policy after the option vintage,
  and CBO's 2026 baseline reports higher projected crop-insurance costs partly
  from continued demand growth. The old score is not current law.
- USDA reports that producers paid 38 percent of premiums on average in 2024,
  compared with the option's proposal to increase their average share to 60
  percent.
- ERS reports $10.4 billion of 2024 premium subsidies, $2.34 billion of
  program-delivery costs, and $2.31 billion of underwriting gains paid to
  approved insurers.
- Participation and indemnities are heterogeneous. Fifteen percent of all
  farms participated in 2024; participation was 62 percent among row-crop
  operations, 19 percent among specialty-crop operations, and 12 percent among
  livestock operations. Large family farms were 18 percent of participants,
  operated 38 percent of harvested cropland, and received 45 percent of
  indemnities.
- GAO estimated that insurers' average 2011-2022 return on retained premiums
  was 16.8 percent versus a 10.2 percent market benchmark. GAO also identifies
  a statutory barrier to lowering expected underwriting gains through the
  Standard Reinsurance Agreement; the matter remained open in February 2026.

## Decision

The broad producer premium reduction remains held. Current participation and
distribution data are baselines, not evidence of how farms would respond to a
roughly 22-point reduction in average federal premium support. Disaster-aid
substitution, resilience, food-security, and current-law score gates also
remain open.

The insurer-compensation component becomes the next narrow analysis target.
It has a stronger independent evidence spine, but it still needs a lawful
mechanism, current AIP cost and competition analysis, service-continuity and
insurer-participation floors, implementation cost, and a matching current-law
score. It is **not admitted savings**; AGR, NET, REV, and rates remain
unchanged.
