# AGR insurer-compensation public-evidence ceiling

The narrower insurer-compensation path is real, but it is legislative—not an
administrative efficiency that USDA can simply collect under the current
Standard Reinsurance Agreement.

## Current-law result

Under 7 U.S.C. 1508, periodic SRA financial renegotiations must remain broadly
budget-neutral. A&O reimbursements are protected relative to the preceding
agreement, and qualifying savings must be recycled into A&O or underwriting
gain payments. Current law also adds, beginning in reinsurance year 2026:

- an additional 6 percent of net book premium for eligible contracts in states
  whose eligible-contract loss ratio exceeds 120 percent;
- a specialty-crop A&O floor of at least 17 percent of the premium used to
  define loss ratio;
- protection against using non-specialty reductions to fund that floor; and
- an A&O inflation adjustment.

The updated 2026 SRA implements that newer perimeter. Therefore, the old
9.25-percent cap and $1.5 billion FY2026 component estimate are not a current
administrative savings path.

## Evidence and service boundary

There is still a legitimate design question. ERS reports $2.34 billion in 2024
program-delivery cost and $2.31 billion in AIP underwriting gains. GAO estimated
a 16.8-percent average return on retained premiums for 2011-2022, compared with
a 10.2-percent market benchmark.

But the public corpus does not supply current AIP-specific allowable costs,
returns, state market shares, agent coverage, or concentration. FCIC reports 12
approved providers for reinsurance year 2026 and gives useful written-agreement
service baselines—an 8.28-percent submission error rate and 5.8-day processing
time—but those figures do not show how compensation changes would affect entry,
exit, adjustment quality, appeals, geographic access, or claim timeliness.

## Decision

The current-law administrative savings path is closed at zero. A bounded
legislative design may now be developed by separating A&O reimbursement from
underwriting gains, defining a reproducible market-return benchmark, protecting
nationwide and specialty/high-loss service, testing insurer and agent response,
including transition costs, and obtaining a current-law score.

No AGR savings are admitted; NET, REV, and rates remain unchanged.
