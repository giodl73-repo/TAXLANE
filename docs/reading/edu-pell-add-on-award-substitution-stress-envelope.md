# EDU Pell Add-On Award and Household Stress

The $1,060 add-on is not a uniform $1,060 loss for every recipient. The new
feature engine replays maximum, SAI-calculated, minimum, threshold, and special-
rule cases. Maximum recipients and several SAI cases lose $1,060, but the
normalized range is $105 to $1,395. The upper result occurs when a current
$1,395 SAI-calculated award falls below the design minimum and the applicant
does not separately qualify for a minimum award.

This exposes a real design issue: simply deleting the add-on can create new
minimum-award and twice-maximum-SAI cliffs. Those rules must be specified in any
legislative alternative rather than hidden inside the old score.

For a maximum recipient, linear exposure is $1,060 at full intensity, $795 at
75 percent, $530 at 50 percent, and $265 at 25 percent. Those are normalized
exposures, not official payment schedules. Household stresses show that a
$1,060 grant loss can remain unmet need, become $530 of additional loans in a
half-loan case, or be divided among loans and institutional aid. The shares are
not empirical forecasts.

No observed 2026–27 recipient distribution yet weights these cases, and no
current institution distribution, borrowing elasticity, cut-specific causal
outcome estimate, or compatible rescore closes the candidate. EDU therefore
moves to named triggers with zero admission, and DIS becomes the next active
owner/outcome lane.
