## Negative Higher-Education Entry

The −$35.005B value is a net federal budget entry. It does not mean government
provided negative education or recovered $35B of fraud or waste.

The sign and amount now have an exact account bridge. Thirty-eight OMB account
rows reconcile to −$35.005B with no difference. Direct Loan credit accounts
contribute −$78.707B: a $64.659B program-account outlay is more than offset by
−$132.927B of negative-subsidy receipts and −$10.439B of downward subsidy
re-estimates. FFEL credit accounts add −$6.752B, while student aid,
administration, institutions, a refundable education tax credit, and smaller
entries offset much of the negative credit-account total. See the
[Higher-Education Account Bridge](higher-education-account-bridge.md).

The Department of Education's FY2025 Agency Financial Report remains useful
for explaining credit re-estimates and loan modifications, but its financial-
statement categories are not substituted for the OMB account rows. The bridge
does not establish that $35.005B of cash was collected or saved.

## Comparison Boundary

Function 500 mixes education, labor, training, and social services. The
[K–12 Federalism Finance Bridge](k12-federalism-finance-bridge.md) now supplies
a consolidated FY2024 school-system frame: $994.922B revenue, split 11.57%
federal, 45.23% state, and 43.20% local. OMB federal outlays must not be added
to that total because Census already includes federal revenue received by
school systems.

The [K–12 Outcome Baseline](k12-outcome-baseline.md) now adds 2024 NAEP public-
school mathematics and reading distributions, the school year 2021–22 adjusted
cohort graduation rate, and school year 2022–23 chronic absenteeism, including
selected subgroup rates. These are descriptive outcomes, not a causal spending-
efficiency score; NAEP Proficient is not grade-level proficiency, national
attendance aggregation can duplicate mobile students, and the finance and
outcome records are not the same cohort.

PISA 2022 supplies the first definition-matched outcome peer: U.S. 15-year-old
scores were 465 versus the OECD average of 472 in mathematics, 504 versus 476
in reading, and 499 versus 485 in science. OECD flags the U.S. estimates because
school participation and student exclusion missed technical targets. PISA
Level 2 is not NAEP Proficient, and the peer outcome is not matched to the
school-system spending frame.

Education at a Glance now adds a matched 2022 resource peer. Government
expenditure per full-time-equivalent student from primary through post-secondary
non-tertiary education was $14,603 PPP-adjusted in the United States versus
$12,438 across the OECD. This does not assign spending to PISA students or
their cumulative schooling inputs and is not a causal efficiency estimate.

The [Census CPS Education Access and Transition Baseline](census-cps-education-access-transition-baseline.md)
adds October 2024 age-enrollment denominators and a recent-high-school-graduate
snapshot. Of 3.250 million people age 15–24 reported as graduating that year,
2.088 million were enrolled in a listed college, graduate, or vocational
category, a derived 64.246% of rounded published counts. These household survey
estimates are not public-school membership, an ACGR cohort, a WIOA Youth
comparison group, or a spending denominator.

The [Pell Short-Training Impact Evidence](pell-short-training-impact-evidence.md)
adds randomized intent-to-treat evidence for two experimental eligibility
offers. Both offers increased study-school enrollment and completion, while
neither produced a statistically detectable medium- to long-term employment or
quarterly earnings effect. The negative labor point estimates are not evidence
of harm or equivalence, and grant amounts among users are not full program costs
or a FY2025 account-outlay crosswalk.

The [FSA Title IV Student Access Baseline](fsa-title-iv-student-access-baseline.md)
adds FY2024 administrative scale: $120,816.1 million disbursed across selected
Title IV programs, including $32,995.7 million for Pell, with aid delivered to
more than 9.9 million students and families. Dollar totals are fiscal-year
amounts while recipient counts are award-year based and qualifier bounded, so
they cannot form an exact cost-per-recipient cohort or FY2025 account crosswalk.

The [Pell Bachelor-Recipient Outcome Baseline](pell-bachelor-recipient-outcome-baseline.md)
adds 2020 employment, worker earnings, federal-loan repayment, and financial-
well-being measures for 2015–16 bachelor completers grouped by lifetime Pell
receipt. The contrasts are observational, COVID-era, and conditioned on degree
completion; they cannot estimate Pell's effect on completing college or be
joined to current FSA recipients, full costs, or FY2025 outlays.

The [BPS First-Time-Student Longitudinal Bridge](bps-first-time-student-longitudinal-bridge.md)
adds an early descriptive cohort of 2019–20 first-time postsecondary entrants
followed at any institution through June 2022, including completers, persisters,
and students no longer enrolled. The separate [Pell Current-Entrant Persistence
Baseline](pell-current-entrant-persistence-baseline.md) now supplies the official
DataLab `PELL20` receipt-group by five-category `PROUT3_NEW` distribution.
Receipt is not eligibility; the early pandemic-era cross-tab is descriptive,
not causal or mature, and cell standard errors do not establish between-group
significance from those cell errors alone. The linked
[significance screen](pell-current-entrant-persistence-significance-screen.md)
preserves DataLab's independent-estimates t-tests: three of five comparisons
pass a derived Bonferroni screen, but covariance-aware confirmation remains
blocked. Neither artifact supplies a
cohort-compatible cost or fiscal link.

The [WIOA National Outcome Baseline](wioa-national-outcome-baseline.md) extends
the card to the worker side of function 500. It records PY2024 national
administrative outcomes for WIOA Adult, Dislocated Worker, Youth, and Wagner-
Peyser participants. These are participant and exit-cohort performance
measures, not population outcomes or estimates of what training caused. The
outcome denominators are not limited to participants recorded as receiving
training, and the Youth employment measures also count education or training.
The record therefore cannot be divided into the $5.434B subfunction-504 outlay
or converted into cost per job, efficiency, fraud, or savings.

The [Training and Employment Account Bridge](training-employment-account-bridge.md)
now reconciles that $5.434B exactly across 13 nonzero DOL/ETA account rows.
DOL budget activities identify FY2025 enacted budget authority for the four
reported WIOA programs, but the OMB actual-outlay rows remain broader account
aggregates. Program-year funding, multi-year grant spending, and lagged exit
cohorts prevent a participant or outcome cost calculation.

The [WIA Gold Standard Impact Evidence](wia-gold-standard-impact-evidence.md)
adds a national randomized design for the predecessor Adult and Dislocated
Worker programs. It supports causal claims for the study's access-to-service
contrasts, including positive intensive-service earnings effects and
inconclusive training evidence. Enrollment occurred in 2011–13 under WIA, not
PY2024 WIOA, so the estimates cannot be transported automatically or joined to
FY2025 outlays.

The [BLS CPS Worker Baseline](bls-cps-worker-baseline.md) adds calendar-year
2024 population, labor-force participation, employment-population, unemployment,
and earnings context by highest educational attainment for people age 25 and
older. The earnings column has a narrower universe—full-time wage and salary
workers—than the employment measures. These cross-sectional associations are
not a WIOA comparison group, do not isolate training effects, and do not close
the subfunction-504 account boundary.

The federalism bridge still cannot be compared directly with an all-government
OECD education percentage. Attach additional access measures with
program-compatible student denominators, program-level actual outlays with
cohort-compatible timing, and current-program causal evidence or a validated
historical-to-current bridge before assigning an expected level.
