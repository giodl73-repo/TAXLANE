# Pell Short-Training Impact Evidence

Machine record:
`data/derived/breadth_benchmark_matrix/pell_short_training_impact_evidence.2012-2021.v1.draft.json`.

The Institute of Education Sciences evaluated two experimental expansions of
Pell Grant eligibility for short occupational training. Forty-six volunteer
postsecondary schools identified 2,684 eligible adults between November 2012
and March 2017. Students had a 60 percent chance of being offered experimental
Pell funds. The randomized estimates therefore measure the intent-to-treat
effect of an offer, not the effect of using a grant, enrolling, completing a
program, or earning a credential.

## Experiment 1: Post-Bachelor's Short Training

Experiment 1 allowed low-income students who already had bachelor's degrees to
use Pell Grants for occupational programs lasting up to one year when attended
full time. The academic sample contained 414 students. Labor-market rows in
Appendix Exhibit C.1 display 250 offered and 156 not-offered observations; the
main report describes the labor-market analytic sample as 407.

| Primary outcome | Offered | Not offered | Impact | SE | p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enrolled at study school | 77.9% | 51.9% | +26.0 pp | 4.0 | <0.001 |
| Completed at study school | 52.4% | 35.6% | +16.7 pp | 4.3 | <0.001 |
| Ever employed, 2020 Q3–2021 Q4 | 80.6% | 83.0% | -2.4 pp | 3.8 | 0.527 |
| Average quarterly wage earnings | $8,956 | $10,097 | -$1,141 | $977 | 0.244 |

## Experiment 2: Very Short Training

Experiment 2 allowed Pell Grants for occupational programs lasting 8 to 15
weeks. Its academic sample contained 2,270 students; employment and earnings
used 2,215 observations.

| Primary outcome | Offered | Not offered | Impact | SE | p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enrolled at study school | 66.4% | 51.8% | +14.6 pp | 1.8 | <0.001 |
| Completed at study school | 47.0% | 37.7% | +9.3 pp | 1.9 | <0.001 |
| Ever employed, 2020 Q3–2021 Q4 | 77.6% | 79.0% | -1.4 pp | 1.8 | 0.424 |
| Average quarterly wage earnings | $5,993 | $6,276 | -$283 | $251 | 0.260 |

Employment and earnings came from the National Directory of New Hires 42 to
93 months after random assignment. Quarterly earnings include zero for people
not employed. The window overlapped the COVID-19 pandemic and recovery.

## Grant Use and Cost Boundary

Among students offered funds, 67 percent used an experimental grant in
Experiment 1 and 52 percent did so in Experiment 2. Average disbursements among
users were $3,577 and $1,312 respectively, or $1,752 across users in both
experiments. These are use and grant-amount observations, not a complete
incremental cost estimate for the randomized offer. They omit other program,
administrative, institutional, household, loan, and opportunity costs and do
not support a cost-effectiveness ratio.

The [FY2024 FSA Title IV baseline](fsa-title-iv-student-access-baseline.md)
provides current administrative program scale, not the experimental-offer
population, control group, outcome cohort, or full cost for this 2012–17 study.

The [B&B Pell bachelor-recipient baseline](pell-bachelor-recipient-outcome-baseline.md)
uses observational lifetime-receipt groups among degree completers. It is not
the experimental-offer population, treatment contrast, or counterfactual here.

## Evidence Boundary

The experiments causally establish that the Pell offers increased enrollment
and completion under the study conditions. Neither experiment detected a
statistically significant medium- to long-term employment or earnings effect.
The negative point estimates are not proof of harm. Nonsignificance also does
not prove equivalence or a precisely zero effect.

Volunteer schools, interested FAFSA-completing applicants, program mix, and
the pandemic-period outcome window limit transportability. The evidence has no
cohort-compatible federal outlay crosswalk and cannot support a current fiscal
score, cost per outcome, fraud finding, or savings estimate.

The [BPS first-time-student longitudinal bridge](bps-first-time-student-longitudinal-bridge.md)
and separate [Pell current-entrant persistence baseline](pell-current-entrant-persistence-baseline.md)
are early descriptive entrant-cohort context, not the experimental offer
population or counterfactual. The DataLab baseline groups recorded receipt,
not eligibility, and its five-category pandemic-era distribution is neither a
causal effect nor a mature outcome. It adds no cost or fiscal link.

The linked [significance screen](pell-current-entrant-persistence-significance-screen.md)
does not create an experimental contrast. Its independent-estimates method has
three of five Bonferroni-screened differences, but covariance-aware
confirmation and causal interpretation remain blocked.
