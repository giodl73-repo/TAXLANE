# SEE energy and weatherization current-law audit

## The candidate was broader than WAP

CBO's December 2024 option reduces new funding for DOE energy-conservation and
weatherization grants through the Office of State and Community Energy Programs
by 25 percent in FY2026 and 50 percent afterward. Its $1.8 billion ten-year
outlay estimate is a portfolio figure; CBO does not publish a WAP component.

Public Law 119-74 subsequently funded WAP for FY2026. DOE's July 2026 notice
records $369 million: $328 million of grantee program and training allocations,
$10 million of headquarters training, $30 million of readiness funding, and
$1 million of other funding. That live WAP perimeter cannot inherit the old
portfolio score.

H.R. 1355 supplies useful later design context but remains reported legislation,
not law. Its authorizations are not appropriations, and CBO scores it as
discretionary cost rather than as a rescore of the reduction option.

## Service and control evidence point in different directions

DOE reports that WAP serves about 32,000 homes annually, supports 8,500 jobs,
and saves served households at least $372 per year on average. It also reports
a 13.9-percent energy burden for low-income households versus 3 percent for
other households and a delivery network of roughly 700 local organizations.
Those figures establish material service and distribution stakes, not the
effect of a future cut.

DOE OIG found serious implementation controls: delayed reporting, unit-cost
exceptions, states with no reported completed units, and Wisconsin-specific
documentation, conflict, complaint, and oversight gaps. Those findings support
a narrower delivery-control successor. They do not establish a cashable amount
or prove programwide failure.

## Decision

The broad portfolio reduction and a WAP-only reduction are both held with zero
admission. The next slice audits a bounded WAP control modernization around
completed units, allowable cost, reporting, documentation, complaints, and
federal oversight. Service recovery, avoided waste, and cashable savings must
remain separate.
