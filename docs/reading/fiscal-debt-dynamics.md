# Fiscal Debt Dynamics, 2026–2036

Machine record:
`data/derived/breadth_benchmark_matrix/fiscal_debt_dynamics_2026_2036.v1.draft.json`.

The CBO February 2026 open-data baseline gives us the annual bridge that the
static fiscal card lacked. Debt held by the public rises from **100.6% of GDP
in 2026 to 120.2% in 2036**. Over the same period, annual net interest rises
from **$1.039T to $2.144T**.

## What the first-order paths show

| Gradual path, fully phased in by 2036 | 2036 primary adjustment | 2036 debt/GDP | Change vs. baseline | 2026–36 primary adjustment | Interest savings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CBO current-law baseline | $0 | 120.2% | — | $0 | $0 |
| Close 1.1% of GDP | $514B | 113.6% | −6.6 points | $2.737T | $354B |
| Reach primary balance | $981B | 107.6% | −12.6 points | $5.225T | $677B |
| Reach 1% primary surplus | $1.448T | 101.6% | −18.7 points | $7.713T | $999B |

The strongest clean headline is not “balance fixes the debt.” It does not.
With a linear phase-in, reaching primary balance by 2036 still leaves debt at
about **107.6% of GDP**. A gradual one-percent primary-surplus path roughly
holds the 2036 debt ratio near today's level rather than returning it to a
historically low level.

## Boundary

These are TAXLANE first-order scenarios, not CBO scores. They keep CBO GDP,
average interest rates, and other financing fixed. Interest savings equal the
current year's CBO average rate times the prior-year reduction in debt. The
paths omit macroeconomic, behavioral, distributional, policy-timing, and
implementation effects. A real policy package must be scored separately.

The official baseline debt identity reconciles within rounding:

```text
ending debt = prior ending debt + total deficit + other financing
```
