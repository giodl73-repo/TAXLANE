# Taxlane result — frequently asked questions

## Is this a proposal to change my taxes?

No. It is an internal analytical recommendation. It is not legislation, an
official proposal, or an individual tax calculation.

## Are 21/23/33/35/43/46/48 my tax rates?

No. They are marginal model bracket rates. A marginal rate applies to income
within a bracket. Your liability would also depend on thresholds, filing status,
deductions, credits, payroll taxes, withholding, refunds, and law not specified
by this schedule.

## Why did Taxlane admit zero spending savings?

The tested candidates did not pass every required source, current-law,
implementation, accounting, interaction, distribution, and outcome-floor gate.
Taxlane does not subtract a headline amount until those gates pass.

## Does zero mean Taxlane found no waste or inefficiency?

No. Zero is the admitted fiscal amount under this decision rule. It is not a
finding that every program is efficient.

## Does zero mean spending can never be reduced?

No. Each track has an evidence-triggered reopening rule. New evidence or a
different, fully specified candidate can change the decision.

## What is the $813.727 billion figure?

It is Taxlane's frozen FY2026 ordinary-income rate-model target after admitted
package effects. It is not total federal receipts, total spending, an official
federal requirement, or a trust-fund target.

## Why not count the $0.4B health or $15B defense headline estimates?

Each retained six unresolved gates. Counting them would assume away access,
quality, readiness, implementation, distribution, interaction, or current-law
questions that the evidence had not resolved.

## Why are Social Security and payment integrity separate?

Social Security uses dedicated payroll and trust-fund finance. Payment integrity
is a cross-program measurement/control layer. Neither is automatically an
ordinary-income package saving.

## Why isn't net interest treated as a cut?

Interest follows borrowing, timing, maturity, and rates. Taxlane recomputes it
from admitted upstream paths instead of declaring a direct cut.

## What is the behavior-robust contingency?

The 22/24/34/36/44/47/49 schedule is the smallest tested one covering all three
taxable-income response cases without added macro stress. It is not the preferred
central recommendation or a guarantee.

## What is the severe stress ceiling?

The +12.6-point schedule covers all nine deliberately adverse combined cases in
Taxlane's test. It is a sensitivity boundary, not a forecast or baseline.

## Does the result balance the federal budget?

No. It is a one-year model result, not a formal all-government balance
certification or ten-year score.

## Does Taxlane account for administrative burden?

Partly. It subtracts a stated $0.077B administration ceiling in the central gap,
but taxpayer, employer, preparer, enforcement, avoidance, and transition burdens
remain incomplete.

## Would a clearer Taxlane receipt make my tax return more complicated?

Not necessarily. A receipt can explain general revenue and public-purpose
allocations without creating new taxes, forms, calculations, or employer
reporting. Taxlane prefers that simpler display path unless a separately
specified policy justifies added compliance burden.

## Who reviewed this?

The repo uses AI-simulated `.roles` and paper-review lenses. They are structured
tests, not real people, institutions, government offices, or endorsements.

## Is this being publicly released?

No. Current work stays inside the repository. Deployment, distribution,
outreach, official requests, and endorsement claims are not authorized.

## Where can I inspect the evidence?

Start with `docs/reading/fifteen-track-terminal-disposition.md`, then the claim
and number ledgers under `docs/explanation/foundation/`. The machine records are
under `data/derived/breadth_benchmark_matrix/`.

The rate-model record identifies Tax-Calculator 6.5.1, its bundled CPS tax-unit
