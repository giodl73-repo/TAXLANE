# The Taxlane result — one-page brief

Label: **Taxlane internal model result and recommendation**
Analysis vintage: **July 27, 2026**
Release state: **repository-only; external release not authorized**

## The result

### Three things to remember

1. Taxlane finished all fifteen tracks but admitted no tested FY2026 spending
   reduction.
2. The $813.727B amount is a Taxlane one-year model target, not the federal
   budget or anyone's tax bill.
3. The seven percentages are marginal model bracket rates selected under stated
   assumptions, not current law or one person's effective rate.

Taxlane reviewed fifteen federal fiscal tracks to ask a disciplined question:
what spending changes can be counted before deciding how much revenue remains
to be raised?

The answer under Taxlane's evidence rules was zero admitted FY2026 primary
spending reduction. That does not mean every program is efficient or that
spending reform is impossible. It means the candidates tested did not clear all
of the source, current-law, implementation, accounting, interaction,
distribution, and service-outcome gates required before a headline estimate
could enter the package.

With zero admitted primary spending reduction, Taxlane retained its frozen
$813.727 billion FY2026 ordinary-income rate-model target. This is not total
federal receipts, total outlays, a Social Security trust-fund requirement, an
official federal target, a total-deficit or debt result, or a literal tracing of
anyone's tax dollars. Federal borrowing remains part of the wider fiscal
picture; this one-year target does not settle it.

Taxlane tested fourteen rate schedules across three taxable-income response
cases—forty-two behavior cases in all. A response case is an assumption about
how reported taxable income changes when rates change; it is not a forecast.
The model used Tax-Calculator 6.5.1 with its bundled CPS tax-unit file for tax
year 2026. Its preferred central analytical
schedule is:

**21 / 23 / 33 / 35 / 43 / 46 / 48 percent**

These are marginal model bracket rates. A marginal rate applies only to taxable
income within that bracket; the schedule is not one person's effective rate or
tax bill. Taxlane does not provide the thresholds, filing-status rules,
deductions, credits, payroll taxes, withholding, or refunds needed to calculate
individual liability.

“Preferred” expresses Taxlane's chosen objective and decision rule. It is a
normative recommendation informed by model results, not a value-free fact.

## The alternatives

- **Preferred central:** 21/23/33/35/43/46/48. Smallest tested one-decimal
  uplift clearing the frozen central case after the administration ceiling.
- **Behavior-robust contingency:** 22/24/34/36/44/47/49. Smallest tested
  schedule covering all three behavior cases without added macro stress.
- **Severe internal stress ceiling:** 22.6/24.6/34.6/36.6/44.6/47.6/49.6.
  First tested schedule covering all nine deliberately adverse combined cases;
  not Taxlane's baseline recommendation or a forecast.

## What happened to the fifteen tracks?

Ten tracks closed at reviewed zero admission. Transportation retained a
conditional cost note. Social Security stayed on a separate dedicated-solvency
rail. Payment integrity stayed non-additive. Revenue retained the internal
planning rate card. Net interest stayed endogenous to admitted debt paths.
Every track has a precise condition that can reopen it.

## Why this matters

Taxlane treats “we found a large number” and “that number can responsibly reduce
the revenue target” as different claims. It also keeps general revenue,
dedicated payroll finance, borrowing, payment-integrity measurements, and net
interest from being added together as though they were interchangeable.
