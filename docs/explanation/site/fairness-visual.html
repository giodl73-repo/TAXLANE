PAY and REV remain analytical overlays rather than spending functions. First-level OECD data cannot consistently separate pensions from family support, highways from energy, or debt service from administration; that is why Taxlane does not manufacture fifteen peer percentages from ten published categories.

Two special accounting tracks do not become life-lane slices: PAY is a control and measurement overlay; REV is the financing system. NET is shown separately as the cost of past borrowing. Peer systems likewise use different audit/control institutions, tax mixes, and debt structures.

The goal

Make the cost of a decent public life visible—then ask whether the US gets comparable outcomes at a fair burden.

The objective is not to force America to equal an OECD average. It is to identify price premiums, missing services, financing mismatches, and debt shifts on comparable bases; preserve explicit service and beneficiary floors; declare who should pay and who should benefit; and only then compute a policy rate.

Taxlane has completed the accounting baseline and built international diagnostics. A peer-adjusted, distributionally fair allocation is the next analytical layer—not a claim already proven.
