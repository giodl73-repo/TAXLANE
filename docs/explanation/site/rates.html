What “marginal” means

Only taxable ordinary income within a bracket is taxed at that bracket's model percentage. The highest listed rate does not apply to all income and is not an average or effective rate. Because the proposal preserves 2026 thresholds and adds 11 points to every ordinary bracket, its narrow bracket-tax difference is 11% of taxable ordinary income before credits and other tax interactions.

What was tested

Tax-Calculator 6.5.1, bundled CPS tax-unit input, tax year 2026; 14 schedules across three taxable-income response cases, or 42 behavior cases. The central schedule's first-year cash proxy is $819.220B; its administration ceiling is $0.077B and reported model gap is $5.416B. These are model quantities, not an official score. Time and debt

One-year cash proxy only. No ten-year official score, complete annual debt path, debt-sustainability certification, or intergenerational result.

Trust funds

Not a solvency certification. OAS remains a separate payroll-financing and adequacy rail; ordinary-income rates do not solve it automatically.

Households and distribution

Incomplete. The CPS model provides distributional outputs, but not a complete household-incidence and benefit analysis.

Compliance and administration

Not fully modeled. Withholding, estimated payments, forms, software, employer transition, filing time, credits, deductions, and private compliance costs require proposal-specific analysis.

What could lower—or raise—the schedule?

Admitted annual spending changes, another financing source, changed model inputs, different behavior, a different distributional objective, administration and compliance costs, macroeconomic feedback, or a changed debt path require a new run. A peer benchmark alone does not change the rate.
