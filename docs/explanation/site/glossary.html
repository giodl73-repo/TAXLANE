Controlled language

Glossary and frequently asked questions

Terms

Admitted reduction
An effect allowed into the package after its source, accounting, service, distribution, implementation, and overlap gates close.
Reviewed zero
A completed conclusion that current evidence supports no admitted amount. It is reopenable.
Primary
Excluding net interest.
Marginal rate
The rate applied only to taxable ordinary income within a bracket, not to all income.
Taxable ordinary income
The ordinary-income amount after applicable deductions used by the bracket calculation; it is not gross salary or total household resources.
Cash proxy
A first-year model output, not an official score or formal balance.
Endogenous
Produced by upstream changes; NET follows an admitted debt path.

FAQ

Does zero mean no reform is possible?

No. It means the tested candidates did not clear the current evidence gates. New evidence, a new candidate, or a changed objective can reopen review.

Are these the rates a person would pay on all income?

No. They are marginal percentages applied in layers to taxable ordinary income. The highest rate does not apply to every dollar.

Are the profile amounts complete tax-return estimates?

No. They show only the ordinary-income bracket calculation for specified taxable ordinary income. They omit deductions, credits, payroll taxes, capital gains, AMT, withholding, refunds, household incidence, and many other rules.

Why does the current Taxlane proposal raise rates?

The frozen scoped target remained $813.727B because no tested FY2026 spending reduction was admitted. The proposal therefore raises all seven ordinary rates by 11 points under its current policy objective. It is an internal result, not law.

Why not lower defense and health spending to peer levels and show lower rates?

Those are legitimate research scenarios, but a spending share is not an executable policy or a savings score. Taxlane still needs matched FY2026 dollars, concrete program changes, readiness or access protections, transition and distribution analysis, and a new microsimulation.

Does peer comparison show that U.S. social services are too high?

No. In the captured 2022 composition, the U.S. social-protection share is below the five displayed peers. Peer alignment might expand or restructure protection rather than cut it.

Is $813.727B the whole federal deficit?

No. It is a scoped remaining FY2026 ordinary-income model target, not total receipts, outlays, the deficit, a trust-fund requirement, or formal balance.

Do payment errors equal savings?

No. PAY is non-additive; owners need net evidence including appeals, burden, implementation, distribution, and overlap.

Does repository readiness authorize publication?

No. Deployment, distribution, outreach, official requests, and endorsement claims remain prohibited without new explicit owner authorization.
