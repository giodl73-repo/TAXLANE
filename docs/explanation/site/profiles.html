Current versus proposed

What would the schedules mean?

Start with the current 2026 ordinary-income schedule, compare Taxlane's evidence-constrained result, and keep peer-rebalancing ideas separate until their spending and service effects are actually scored.

The honest headline

The present Taxlane proposal is not a lower-rate proposal. Because no tested spending reduction cleared every evidence and service gate, the preferred model raises each of the seven ordinary-income marginal rates by 11 percentage points. Lower rates require admitted—not assumed—changes to the spending target, another financing source, a different policy objective, or a combination of those choices.

What peer alignment actually suggests

Not every lane points toward a cut

Defense: lower-share research case↓ possible

The U.S. captured defense share is above every displayed peer. A lower target is plausible to study, but readiness, posture, alliances, veterans, transition, timing, and local economic effects must close before savings enter a rate model.

Health: cost-convergence case↓ possible

The U.S. captured government health share is above every displayed peer. The credible question is whether prices and administration can fall while coverage, access, quality, rural capacity, and household burden hold.

Social protection: not a simple cut↑ / ↔

The U.S. captured social-protection share is below the five displayed peers. Peer alignment would more likely expand or restructure retirement, disability, family, and unemployment protection than automatically reduce it.

No rate is assigned directly to a country-share comparison. The international table is 2022 general government; Taxlane's current rate target is FY2026 federal. The ladder below instead asks what the Taxlane rate would be if explicit net-savings goals were achieved. A defensible policy still needs a matched-year dollar crosswalk, a concrete package, service floors, distribution and transition analysis, and a fresh microsimulation.

A budget is a cycle, not a permanent ranking

Measure → protect floors → diagnose pain and excess → rank specific interventions → allocate → deliver and monitor → sunset or continue → rebalance.

Balance

Use obligations, outcomes, common-perimeter peer context, historical trends, and whole-system costs to find structural imbalance.

Fix pain

Direct temporary or structural money toward the most severe, urgent, remediable harms—not automatically toward the largest department or peer gap.

Learn

Measure the next dollar's effect, delivery capacity, equity, displacement, and household or state-local cost shifts.

Rebalance again

Sunset temporary funds, stop weak interventions, preserve working commitments, and return released money to the next all-lane review.

Education is not pre-funded. Its captured U.S. general-government share is already at the top of the displayed peer range. It can still win reserve funding when a specific, evidenced education pain and intervention outrank alternatives.

How the reserve eventually moves

Ten gates first

Current pain and population, a real severity target, a named intervention and owner, marginal outcome, whole-system cost, distribution, delivery, overlap, duration, and policy/stress floor passage.

Visible scoring

Pain 25% · population 15% · urgency 15% · marginal outcome 25% · equity 10% · net value 10%, discounted by evidence strength and delivery confidence.

Allocation controls

Keep 10% for contingencies, cap a lane at 30% of the allocatable reserve, honor intervention capacity, and leave unsupported dollars unallocated.

Challenge the weights

Repeat with equal, pain-heavy, and outcome-heavy weights. Structural base funding requires stability across those sensitivity cases.

The practical pattern: reinvestment and net savings can coexist. As the assumed net savings rise from $150B to $600B, the modeled ordinary-income uplift falls from 8.9 points to 2.8 points. That is the conditional rate dividend; it becomes real only if the spending package becomes real.

What “owner reforms” means

Payment integrity, procurement, subsidy, justice, and international operating changes count only when the responsible program owner can attribute an annual net effect. PAY remains non-additive: an error estimate cannot be added on top of the same program's saving.

The path to lower rates

What has to be true before the rate can come down

1 · Choose a concrete change

“Spend like peers” is a benchmark, not a policy. Name programs, prices, contracts, eligibility, timing, and implementation.

2 · Protect outcomes

Demonstrate readiness, access, quality, benefits, rights, resilience, or other applicable service floors.

3 · Reconcile net savings

Subtract transition, administration, behavior, overlap, state/local substitution, and financing effects.

4 · Re-run profiles and rates

Reduce the scoped target only by the admitted annual amount, then repeat the microsimulation and household analysis.

Not a tax estimate

These profiles do not determine any person's complete liability and are not tax, legal, or accounting advice. The Taxlane schedules are internal model scenarios, not law or an official score. Direct country-share scenarios retain null rates; the four policy-profile rates are conditional on unscored, unadmitted net-savings goals.
