# The civic question

How can spending evidence and revenue modeling share one auditable accounting
spine without converting every idea into claimed savings? [CLM-01]

Speaker note: Ask readers to separate public purpose, cost measurement, an
admission decision, and a financing recommendation.

---

# The conclusion in 30 seconds

Taxlane reviewed 15 tracks, admitted **$0.000B** in FY2026 primary spending
reductions, preserved a scoped **$813.727B** ordinary-income model target, and
selected a preferred analytical marginal schedule of
**21/23/33/35/43/46/48**. [NUM-01, NUM-03, NUM-04, NUM-08]

Speaker note: “Primary” excludes net interest. The target is not a whole-budget
balance statement.

---

# Why zero is a result

A candidate must be current, scored on the right fiscal object, net of costs
and overlaps, and bounded by beneficiary, distribution, service-continuity,
implementation, and compliance evidence. [CLM-03]

Speaker note: A headline estimate can be useful context and still fail the
admission rule.

---

# Public purposes stay visible

The tracks cover mobility; health and access; learning; retirement adequacy;
food and family stability; veterans; agriculture; defense; disasters; justice;
science, energy, and environment; diplomacy; payment integrity; revenue; and
debt costs.

Speaker note: A zero admission is not a zero valuation of those purposes.

# PAY–NET–REV identity

1. PAY detects and measures, but program owners book any supported net effect.
2. NET is recomputed from an admitted annual debt path.
3. REV models the remaining scoped financing target.

Speaker note: This prevents double counting and invented interest savings.
[CLM-05]

---

# From target to preferred rate

The model tested 14 uniform-uplift schedules across three taxable-income
response cases—42 behavior cases. [NUM-05, NUM-06]

The preferred 11-point uplift produces an **$819.220B** central first-year cash
proxy against the target, with a **$5.416B** model gap after the **$0.077B**
administration ceiling. [NUM-09, NUM-10, NUM-11]

Speaker note: Cash proxy and model gap are not formal budget balance.

---

# Marginal is not effective

Under a marginal schedule, only income within each bracket is taxed at that
bracket's percentage. The highest listed model rate is not applied to all of a
person's income. [CLM-06]

Speaker note: The corpus does not estimate household distribution, incidence,
or filing burden comprehensively.

# Beneficiaries and implementation

Reopening a spending candidate requires a credible shortfall and
service-continuity rule where people or legal commitments rely on the program.
Owner-attributed net effects must include appeals, burden, take-up,
implementation cost, distribution, and overlap evidence. [CLM-09, CLM-10]

Speaker note: “Improper” does not automatically mean recoverable cash.

---

# Time and debt limits

The rate output is a first-year FY2026 analytical result. It is not a ten-year
score, a trust-fund solvency proof, or a debt-sustainability certification.
NET remains endogenous until an annual debt path exists. [CLM-07]

Speaker note: Separate current-year arithmetic from long-run claims.

---

# What would change the result?

- a current enactment or score changes a candidate's basis;
- owner-level evidence closes the relevant gates;
- an admitted annual path triggers NET recomputation;
- upstream effects or model inputs change the remaining target; or
- the decision-maker changes the policy objective. [CLM-08]

Speaker note: Reopening is disciplined revision, not automatic approval.
