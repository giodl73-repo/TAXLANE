Taxlane in five minutes

Repository-only briefing source

2026-07-27

What question did Taxlane ask?

What federal services cost, what evidence supports changing those costs, and what ordinary-income rate schedule follows when unsupported savings are excluded. [CLM-01]

Speaker note: This is an analytical framework, not an official budget or a proposal authorized for public release.

The result

  • 15 tracks examined. [NUM-01]
  • 10 reviewed zero-admission tracks. [NUM-02]
  • $0.000B admitted FY2026 primary spending reduction. [NUM-03]
  • $813.727B frozen remaining ordinary-income model target. [NUM-04]

Speaker note: Zero means the tested candidates did not clear their gates. It does not say no reform could ever work or that every program is efficient.

Speaker note: OAS, PAY, REV, and NET are not comparable spending-cut rows and receive zero additive package credit. [CLM-04]

One accounting spine

PAY measures; owners book. NET follows debt. REV finances the remainder.

No PAY amount may be double-counted as owner-program savings. No NET effect is claimed without an admitted upstream annual debt path. [CLM-05]

Speaker note: The $813.727B target is a scoped model target, not total federal receipts, total outlays, or a trust-fund requirement.

What would change the result?

New enacted policy, current scores, owner-attributed net effects, closed beneficiary and service-continuity gates, changed model inputs, or a changed policy objective can reopen a track or rate rail. [CLM-08]

Speaker note: Reopening restarts review; it does not guarantee admission.

Caveat and evidence route

Taxlane is an internal analytical recommendation using Tax-Calculator 6.5.1, the bundled CPS tax-unit input, and tax year 2026. It is not enacted law, personal tax advice, an official score, or formal proof of balance. [CLM-02] It is also not a complete household distribution, incidence, filing-burden, or taxpayer compliance-burden analysis. [CLM-10, CLM-12]
