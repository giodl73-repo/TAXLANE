# Efficiency Pressure Framework

## Purpose

Large outlays create pressure to ask whether money is being spent well. They do
not, by themselves, prove waste. TAXLANE should therefore separate three things:

1. **Outlay pressure**: the category is large enough that small efficiency gains
   matter.
2. **Benchmark pressure**: the category is high versus peers, history, or
   outcome-adjusted expectations.
3. **Waste finding**: a reviewed GAO, inspector-general, CBO, agency, court, or
   audit source identifies improper payments, duplication, unnecessary cost,
   failed controls, or poor performance.

TaxLane can publish pressure and questions now. It cannot publish a waste
finding until the source and wording pass accountability review.

## Waste vs. efficiency

| Label | Meaning | Public-use status |
|---|---|---|
| High outlay | The category is large. | Safe if sourced. |
| Efficiency pressure | The category deserves cost/performance scrutiny. | Safe if caveated. |
| Savings opportunity | A specific lever could lower cost while preserving the public purpose. | Requires evidence and outcome floor. |
| Waste risk | A source indicates possible improper, duplicative, unnecessary, or low-value spending. | Requires reviewed source and careful wording. |
| Waste finding | A competent source has made a finding. | Requires exact citation and role-reviewed public wording. |

## Current pressure surfaces

| Surface | Why it deserves pressure | What would drive it down over time |
|---|---|---|
| Health and Medicare | Largest benchmark gap; US pays roughly twice peer compulsory/government health cost with worse coverage/outcomes. | Price, administrative overhead, provider payment, drug pricing, billing complexity, and outcome-per-dollar reforms with coverage floors. |
| Interest on debt | Very large current outlay caused by accumulated borrowing and rates. | Primary deficit reduction, debt/GDP stabilization, maturity/rate risk management; not a program cut. |
| Defense | Large and above European norm, but strategic obligations matter. | Procurement discipline, readiness-per-dollar, audit controls, base/force posture review, and clear strategy-cost linkage. |
| Disaster relief and insurance | Event-driven and lumpy; can hide long-run exposure growth. | Mitigation, insurance pricing, land-use incentives, resilient infrastructure, and clearer supplemental-budget tracking. |
| Payment integrity and administration | Smaller than the main lanes but directly connected to public trust. | Improper-payment controls, eligibility accuracy, claims timeliness, audit follow-through, and simpler administration. |

## Operating rule

For each pressure surface, TAXLANE should track:

- outlay scale,
- why pressure exists,
- the evidence needed,
- the cost-down lever,
- the outcome or legal-obligation floor,
- whether a public claim is allowed.

The default public claim status is blocked.

## Practical conclusion

The strongest current "drive down over time" target is health cost per outcome.
The second is debt interest, but the lever is fiscal balance rather than a
program-efficiency audit. Defense and disaster spending deserve scrutiny, but
they require strategy and event context before calling the level too high.
