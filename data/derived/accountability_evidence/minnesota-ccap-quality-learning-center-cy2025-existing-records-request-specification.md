## Existing-Records Target

The draft requests only existing provider-level payment ledgers, exports,
reports, query outputs, calculation records, data dictionaries, cutoff
snapshots, and reconciliations already maintained for license 1087038 and
CY2025. It seeks existing service-period, posting-date, amount, transaction-
type, adjustment, reversal, void, reissue, status, extract-date, and aggregation
fields where already maintained.

It does not ask DCYF to answer questions, create a record, run a new query,
build a custom export, calculate $1.9 million, perform analysis, or construct a
new reconciliation. Existing component records are acceptable when no single
reconciliation record exists.

## Route and Authority

DCYF's official request page and public-data guide say public-data requests
must be written, need no required form, should identify the Minnesota
Government Data Practices Act and clearly describe the data, and may be sent to
`dcyf.datarequest@state.mn.us` or the listed postal address. Minnesota Statutes
sections 13.03 and 142E.02 supply government-data access and CCAP administration
context. They do not establish that each requested record or field exists, is
public, or must be released.

The draft requires exactly one channel after owner authorization. It does not
claim a fee waiver, expedited processing, guaranteed response time, or a right
to a particular custom format.

## Privacy and Classification Boundary

The request excludes child, family, recipient, caregiver, and staff direct
identifiers; case, eligibility, benefit, Social Security, and taxpayer IDs;
attendance details; personal contact and free-text data; banking information;
and credentials. It accepts provider-level aggregates, lawful redaction, or
redacted or deidentified transaction rows.

The request does not predict classification or release. A no-records response,
denial, classification, clarification request, cost estimate, or redaction is
an administrative event, not evidence for or against the attributed amount.
Any future production requires custody, privacy review, cutoff and transaction
reconciliation, recipient-response review, and separate evidence adoption.
