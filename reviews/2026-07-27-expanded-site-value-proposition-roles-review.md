# Expanded Taxlane site and value proposition — `.roles` review

Date: 2026-07-27
Scope: eight-page repository-local site, including the expanded homepage,
library, fifteen-track surface, rate explanation, method, evidence routes,
FAQ, taxpayer-receipt visual, and six-country comparison
Nature: AI-simulated role review; not endorsement, professional advice, an
official score, or public-release authorization

## Executive verdict

The panel finds a **strong and differentiated value proposition that is not yet
fully accepted as a taxpayer-facing package**.

Taxlane's winning claim is not that it has discovered the uniquely correct tax
rate. Its winning claim is that it has built a controlled route from public
purpose to evidence-admitted spending effects to a modeled financing result:

> **Taxlane is an evidence-control system for fiscal claims. It refuses to count
> savings until sources, accounting, beneficiaries, timing, implementation, and
> overlap reconcile—then shows the rate implication of what remains.**

That proposition is unusual, legible, and defensible. It becomes weaker when
the large bracket schedule or repository activity counts appear to be the main
product. The rate is a result of the method; the pulse count is evidence of
work performed; neither should displace the public value of preventing false
savings, double counting, hidden borrowing, and unsupported certainty.

Panel disposition: **1 P1, 5 P2, 2 P3; revise before a second-round acceptance
review.** Existing external-release restrictions remain unchanged.

## Is it a winning value proposition?

**Yes, for the right promise and audience.**

The strongest audiences are serious citizens, educators, fiscal researchers,
policy analysts, journalists, and maintainers who need to distinguish a
plausible budget claim from an admissible one. For them, Taxlane offers a clear
benefit: every simplified conclusion can be reopened, audited, and updated
without mixing unlike accounting objects.

The proposition is not yet winning if presented as “Taxlane solved the federal
budget” or “these are the correct national tax rates.” That framing outruns the
absence of an official score, a ten-year balance path, complete household
incidence, full compliance modeling, and a same-source detailed international
crosswalk.

Recommended public-facing hierarchy:

1. **Problem:** budget debates count attractive savings before proving them.
2. **Product:** Taxlane tests every claim through a shared evidence and
   accounting spine.
3. **Benefit:** unsupported savings and double counts stay out; public purposes
   and affected people stay visible.
4. **Result:** under the current scoped evidence, $0.000B was admitted and the
   $813.727B ordinary-income model target remained.
5. **Rate implication:** the preferred internal marginal schedule is one model
   result, with behavior and stress alternatives.
6. **Boundary:** the result is reopenable analysis, not law, an official score,
   or proof that every program is efficient.

## Second-round acceptance gates

Before the expanded site receives full eight-lens acceptance:

1. Create the international-comparison source/calculation record and evidence
   route.
2. Put marginal-rate and scoped-target explanations adjacent to the hero result.
3. Add concrete taxpayer and beneficiary examples.
4. Put the one-year, compliance, distribution, debt, and trust-fund limitations
   beside the rate result.
5. Reframe corpus counts as supporting inventory and lead with user-visible
   outcomes.
6. Re-run all eight lenses, local link/accessibility checks, the 388-test suite,
   domain validation, and manifest validation.
